On-Campus

  • Most CS grad students live off campus — halls are undergrad-oriented and grad students often need a manual portal override to apply (studenthousing@utsa.edu).
  • Every UT San Antonio Housing resident must buy a meal plan. None is selected by move-in, one is auto-assessed.
  • University Oaks is the sole exception — apartment-style, leased through Campus Living Villages, no meal plan required.
  • Main Campus halls: Alvarez, Blanco, Chaparral Village, Chisholm, Guadalupe, Laurel Village. Chisholm has private baths; Chaparral and Laurel are suite-style with kitchenettes.
  • The Continental (322 W Commerce St) is the only UT San Antonio housing at the Downtown Campus.
  • Utilities and internet included; academic-year leases; billed to the student account, so financial aid applies.
  • Options and applications: housing.utsa.edu

Getting In from the Airport

SAT is about 15 miles from Main Campus. Rideshare from Terminal A lower-level commercial curb is ~25 min; the GO Airport Shuttle desk at baggage claim is ~40 min; VIA routes 7 → 93 is cheapest at ~1 hr 20 min but involves a transfer — hard with a semester's luggage. Arrange late-night and weekend arrivals in advance. SAT ground transportation

Parking

  • Grad students on a GRA/GTA appointment are employees and normally buy Employee B; without an appointment the equivalent is Commuter Student (C). Employee B also parks in Employee A spaces evenings and weekends.
  • Buy through the MyParking portal. Permit types and rates
  • Where CS grads park: the lots beside NPB on Main; D1 or D2 downtown, under the elevated highway, with the Little Runner covering the last leg to San Pedro. Downtown parking
  • No permit needed 10pm–7am Mon–Thu and 4:30pm Friday to 7am Monday. Hourly parking via ParkMobile. Parking guide

Driving in Texas

  • Licence: Texas DPS · registration and annual inspection: Texas DMV
  • Liability insurance is legally required. International students should check with ISSS before applying for a licence — requirements depend on visa status and SSN.

💰 Banking and Finance

Informational only — CSGRAD does not endorse any bank or card, and none of this is financial advice. Terms and fees change constantly; check the provider before acting.

Opening an Account

  • Open one in your first weeks — stipends, payroll and refunds all need a US account.
  • Typically bring: passport, I-20 or DS-2019, visa and I-94, proof of UT San Antonio enrollment, and a local address.
  • Some banks open a checking account without an SSN using your immigration documents; others require one. Policies vary by bank and by branch — call ahead rather than making the trip twice.
  • Expect to book an appointment. Most branches will not open a new account as a walk-in, and appointment slots fill up in August when everyone arrives at once.
  • ISSS publishes a banking guide for international students. New student arrival guide · International.Services@utsa.edu

  • Both branches sit near De Zavala Rd and IH-10, southeast of Main Campus — not walkable, and not on a Runner route.
  • Age-based fee waivers miss a lot of grad students — check which account you actually qualify for rather than assuming the student one.
  • Branches matter for wire transfers, cashier's checks and notarization. A branchless account is hard to use alone in your first year.

New Account Offers

  • All three run new-customer cash bonuses, typically requiring either a set number of qualifying transactions or a direct deposit within the first 45–60 days.
  • Wells Fargo excludes non-resident aliens and anyone filing IRS Form W-8, which rules out most F-1 students in their early years — read the eligibility terms before counting on a bonus.
  • Amounts and deadlines change monthly. NerdWallet and Credit Karma track current offers across providers.

Credit Cards

Building a US credit history early makes renting, phone plans, car loans and insurance easier later.

  • Most issuers require an SSN or ITIN. Without one, a secured card — where your deposit becomes the credit line — is usually the only route in.
  • Student cards are designed for applicants with limited or no credit history, and the common ones carry no annual fee.
  • Some cards are poorly accepted outside the US, which matters if you travel home.
  • Compare current cards and terms on NerdWallet or Credit Karma.

  • You are billed unless a waiver is approved — submitting is not enough.
  • Windows are narrow and open before the term starts (fall runs roughly mid-July to mid-September). Current dates and the comparable-coverage criteria: Waiver portal

✈️ Travel Funding

Graduate School Academic Travel Fund (GSATF)

Supplemental funding to attend a major professional conference where you are presenting. It is designed to top up departmental support, not replace it.

Eligibility

  • Enrolled in 6+ credit hours in fall or spring (1–3 doctoral dissertation hours count), or 3+ in summer.
  • Good academic standing both in the semester you are awarded and the semester you use the funds.
  • One award per academic year.
  • No more than 3 student authors per project can be funded.
  • Presenting counts broadly — papers, posters, panels and roundtables, including moderating.

Deadlines

SemesterSubmit by
FallSeptember 1 · November 1
SpringFebruary 1 · April 1
SummerJuly 1

Fixed dates, not rolling. Allow 30 days past the deadline for review and disbursement.

Applying

  • Several travel authorization and release forms go to your department first — start there, since collecting signatures is the slow part.
  • The application needs a PI Acknowledgement signature and a 500–750 word personal statement.
  • Up to $750 per student.
  • Reimbursement only — you pay up front and are repaid after the trip. Budget for that gap, especially on international travel.
  • Colleges disburse the funds; anything unused returns to the Graduate School.
  • Graduate School Academic Travel Fund

Other Sources

  • Your advisor's grant is usually the primary funder — ask there first, since GSATF is explicitly supplemental.
  • Department and college travel support.
  • Conference travel grants — ACM, IEEE and most major venues run their own student travel awards. Apply as soon as your paper is accepted; these deadlines are often earlier than the university's.

Before You Book

  • Get the departmental travel authorization approved before you travel — reimbursement depends on it.
  • Keep everything — receipts for flights, ground transport, meals and accommodation, plus boarding passes and every confirmation and booking email.
  • Save them as you go rather than at the end; hotel folios and meal receipts are the ones people lose.
  • International students: confirm your travel signature and visa status with ISSS before leaving the country.
