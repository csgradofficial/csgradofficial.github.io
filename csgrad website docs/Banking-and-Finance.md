# Banking and Finance

Informational only — CSGrad does not endorse any bank or card, and none of this is financial advice. Terms and fees change constantly; check the provider before acting.

## Opening an Account

- Open one in your first weeks — stipends, payroll and refunds all need a US account
- Typically bring: **passport, I-20 or DS-2019, visa and I-94, proof of UTSA enrollment, and a local address**
- Some banks open a checking account **without an SSN** using your immigration documents; others require one. Policies vary by bank and by branch — call ahead rather than making the trip twice
- **Expect to book an appointment.** Most branches will not open a new account as a walk-in, and appointment slots fill up in August when everyone arrives at once
- ISSS publishes a banking guide for international students · [new student arrival guide](https://global.utsa.edu/students/new-student-arrival-guide.html) · International.Services@utsa.edu

- Age-based fee waivers miss a lot of grad students — check which account you actually qualify for rather than assuming the student one
- Branches matter for wire transfers, cashier's checks and notarization. A branchless account is hard to use alone in your first year

## New Account Offers

- All three run new-customer cash bonuses, typically requiring either a set number of qualifying transactions or a direct deposit within the first 45–60 days
- **Wells Fargo excludes non-resident aliens and anyone filing IRS Form W-8**, which rules out most F-1 students in their early years — read the eligibility terms before counting on a bonus
- Amounts and deadlines change monthly. [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/) and [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/) track current offers across providers — better than checking each bank one at a time

## Credit Cards

Building a US credit history early makes renting, phone plans, car loans and insurance easier later.

- Most issuers require an **SSN or ITIN**. Without one, a **secured card** — where your deposit becomes the credit line — is usually the only route in
- **Student cards** are designed for applicants with limited or no credit history, and the common ones carry no annual fee
- Some cards are poorly accepted outside the US, which matters if you travel home
- Compare current cards and terms on [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/) or [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/)
